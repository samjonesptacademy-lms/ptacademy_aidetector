# Design handoff — Assessor training deck

**Deliverable:** a training deck teaching PT Academy assessors how to read the updated
AI Detection Report.
**Audience:** internal assessors. Subject-matter experts in fitness qualifications,
not in AI or statistics. Assume no technical background and no prior briefing.
**Tone:** practical and calm. This deck teaches a workflow, not a warning system.

---

## 1. Why this deck exists

The AI detection tool changed substantially. Two things assessors relied on are gone,
and one new thing has appeared:

| Change | Assessor impact |
|---|---|
| The overall portfolio **percentage was removed** | They will look for it and not find it |
| Reports now open with a **Portfolio Verdict** (words, not a number) | New concept to learn |
| **Flagged sentences** now appear under answers | New, and the most useful thing in the report |

**The single most important idea in the deck:** the tool tells you *what to read*.
It does not tell you what the learner did. Every design and wording decision should
reinforce that.

---

## 2. The four things assessors must leave knowing

1. **Follow the Verdict.** It is the primary signal.
2. **The AI Score is secondary and can disagree with the Verdict.** That is expected,
   not a fault.
3. **Flagged sentences show exactly what to read.** Start there.
4. **This is indicative, never proof.** It prompts a conversation, not a sanction.

If the deck achieves nothing else, it must land #2 and #4. #2 is the one that generates
support questions; #4 is the one that carries risk if misunderstood.

---

## 3. Content — Portfolio Verdict

Every report opens with one of five. Use the exact wording; it appears verbatim in the
product.

| Verdict | Meaning | Action | Colour |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Detailed Review Required** | Widespread AI indicators — review the whole portfolio. | Review everything, not just flagged answers. Consider verifying understanding through questioning or practical assessment. | `#d03b3b` |
| **Review Flagged Answers** | AI indicators found in specific answers. | Read each flagged answer and judge whether the writing matches the learner's work elsewhere. | `#ec835a` |
| **Spot-Check Suggested** | Minor indicators only; no outright AI verdicts. | A spot-check of flagged answers is sufficient. | `#c6a906` |
| **No AI Indicators Found** | Every assessed answer was classified as human-written. | No action. | `#2a78d6` |
| **Not Enough Assessable Text** | Too few answers could be assessed to score the portfolio. | Treat verdicts as indicative only; rely on your own judgement. | `#9a9a94` |

Note the wording pattern — every verdict names **an action**, never a conclusion about
the learner. The deck should make this explicit at least once: we deliberately do not
say "High AI Content Detected", because a wrong review costs an assessor some reading
whereas a wrong accusation costs a great deal more.

---

## 4. Content — per-answer classifications

| Badge | Means | Colour |
|---|---|---|
| **AI** | Judged AI-generated | `#d03b3b` |
| **MIXED** | Partly AI-generated | `#ec835a` |
| **HUMAN + AI PARTS** | Human-written but may contain AI parts | `#ec835a` |
| **HUMAN** | Human-written | `#2a78d6` |
| **NOT ASSESSED** | Too little text to judge reliably | `#9a9a94` |

Flagged answers are listed **most serious first** (AI → Mixed → Human + AI parts), so
the review queue is already prioritised. Worth a slide — assessors used to unit order
will notice.

---

## 5. Content — the three signals (the critical slide)

This is the concept most likely to be misread. Give it a full slide, ideally with a
worked example.

| Signal | Role | What to know |
|---|---|---|
| **Verdict** | **Primary** | The overall assessment of the answer. Follow this one. |
| **AI Score %** | **Secondary** | How much of the answer reads as AI-generated. **Not a probability.** Measured separately from the Verdict. |
| **Flagged sentences** | Evidence | The specific sentences identified, shown in red under the answer. Quickest way into a review. |

**The point to hammer:** the Verdict and the AI Score come from two independent checks,
so they can disagree.

- An answer can show **AI Score 0%** with a **MIXED** verdict.
- An answer can show **AI Score 100%** with a human-leaning verdict.
- An answer can be flagged with **no flagged sentences at all**.

None of these is an error. **Where they differ, follow the Verdict.**

A real example worth using — from the sample portfolio, an answer scored `24%` with the
verdict *"Most Likely Human written, may include parts generated by AI/GPT"* and one
flagged sentence: *"Hypertension and diabetes place pressure on healthcare services and
can reduce quality of life."* Generic textbook phrasing, exactly what an assessor should
eyeball. Under the old logic this answer was reported as plain "Human" with nothing to
look at.

---

## 6. Content — limits and cautions

Must appear, ideally as its own slide near the end:

- AI detection is **indicative, not conclusive**. Results identify answers worth reading
  closely. They are **not evidence of misconduct on their own**.
- Weigh against the learner's other work and your professional judgement.
- Detection is **less reliable on short answers** (under 50 words). On these portfolios
  most answers are short — one sample had 74 of 76 under 50 words.
- **NOT ASSESSED means no result, not a pass.** Those answers were too short to judge and
  are excluded from all counts.
- A flagged answer is the **start of a conversation with the learner**, not an outcome.

---

## 7. Suggested structure (9–10 slides)

1. **Title** — What's changed in your AI reports
2. **Why** — what the old percentage was, and why it was misleading
3. **The new front page** — annotated screenshot of the report summary
4. **The five Portfolio Verdicts** — table from §3, colour-coded
5. **The three signals** — §5. The most important slide
6. **When they disagree** — worked example, the 0%-but-Mixed case
7. **Flagged sentences** — annotated screenshot, "start here"
8. **Reading order** — flagged first, most serious first; routine answers listed at the back
9. **Limits** — §6
10. **What to do next** — where to find this again (Help in the tool); who to ask

## 8. Visual direction

**Brand:** PT Academy — gold `#c6a906` on near-black `#030303`. Logo at
`logo.png` in the repo root.

**Report palette** (use these exact values so the deck matches what assessors see):

| Role | Hex |
|---|---|
| Critical / AI | `#d03b3b` |
| Attention / Mixed | `#ec835a` |
| Clear / Human | `#2a78d6` |
| Neutral / Not assessed | `#9a9a94` |
| Brand gold | `#c6a906` |
| Surface tint | `#f7f6f2` |
| Hairline | `#dedbd2` |

**Important:** blue is used for "Human", not green. This is deliberate — green against
the attention orange fails protanopia separation (ΔE 5.6), so roughly 1 in 12 men could
not distinguish them. The palette was validated for colour-vision deficiency. **Do not
substitute green for blue anywhere in the deck**, including in charts or tick marks.

Colour encodes **review priority**, not classification — which is why MIXED and
HUMAN + AI PARTS share the same orange. Always pair colour with its text label; never
rely on colour alone.

## 11. Please avoid

- **Any overall percentage or score for a portfolio.** Deliberately removed; reintroducing
  it in the deck would undo the main change.
- **Language implying proof or guilt** — "caught", "cheating", "proven". The product
  says "indicates" and "worth reviewing"; the deck should match.
- **Green for "Human"** — see §8.
- **Framing the AI Score as a probability or confidence level.** It is neither.
- **Implying NOT ASSESSED is a clean result.** It means no result.
